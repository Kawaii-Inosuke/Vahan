Why We Exist

India has 40 crore+ blue-collar workers, and most still find work the way their parents did: asking friends, cousins, agents. Not because technology hasn’t reached them, 80 crore Indians carry smartphones, but because when your livelihood is at stake, you want to talk to a human.

Vahan began in 2016 with a voice you could call. Her name was Laxmi, a virtual teacher who taught English over an ordinary phone call. She taught us the lesson the company is built on: technology works for Bharat when it talks. We moved to WhatsApp, pivoted from teaching to jobs, and built the bot that brought work to lakhs. Today we have come full circle: an AI recruiter that calls you, speaks your language, and stays with you until your first day of work.

The mission has never changed: bring opportunities to people. Jobs first. In time, everything a livelihood needs. The north star: earning opportunities for a billion people.

The Journey

2016

Founded in Bengaluru. The first product is Laxmi, a virtual English teacher you could call on any phone.

2019

Y Combinator (S19), and the pivot to jobs. Among the first companies on the WhatsApp API. Airtel invests through its Startup Accelerator, opening a reach of 30 crore subscribers.

2022

Named a Technology Pioneer by the World Economic Forum.

2024

$10M Series B led by Khosla Ventures, with Y Combinator and Vijay Shekhar Sharma participating.

2025

OpenAI backs Vahan. Persol Group and Temasek’s LemmaTree invest. The voice AI recruiter goes to work at national scale.

2026

Vahan turns 10. 20 lakh+ workers placed, 20K+ every month, across 900+ cities, with a 5 crore+ worker database.

The People Behind It

Madhav Krishna, Founder and CEO.

Computer engineering in Delhi, AI at Columbia, and seven years building in US startups before coming home for what he calls a once-in-many-generations chance: 80 crore Indians online, and life-changing products for India’s masses still unbuilt. He runs Vahan on a simple test: with five minutes left to live, would you be proud of what you’re doing today? Vahan, he says, is hopefully the last job he’ll ever do.
